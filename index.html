Evolutionary genomics · Population genomics

I study how biodiversity arises, persists, and changes through evolutionary processes.

I am an evolutionary genomicist and postdoctoral researcher at the University of Edinburgh. My research examines speciation, which generates diversity between lineages; hybridisation, which transfers and reshuffles variation between them; and selection and other population processes that determine the fate of variation in natural populations. I am particularly interested in how genomic architecture, gene flow, selection and demography interact to shape the origin, persistence, and loss of variation in natural populations.

Background

I completed my PhD at the University of Helsinki in 2024. My doctoral work focused on hybridisation, introgression and reproductive isolation in a young wood-ant species group.

I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Edinburgh, supported by a personal grant from the Finnish Cultural Foundation. Alongside research, I am enthusiastic about teaching.

I am developing an independent research programme in evolutionary and population genomics, with a particular interest in how genomic architecture interacts with environmental change.
